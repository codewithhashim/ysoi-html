The living tradition of Ashtanga & Hatha Yoga

Our yoga school history

Rooted in an unbroken family tradition of classical yoga, our teachings carry forward the timeless wisdom, discipline and spiritual heritage of generations of traditional Yoga Masters, connecting the sacred traditions of India with the practice of authentic yoga in Australia.

Yoga and Meditation School of India was established in Melbourne in 2010. It teaches traditional Ashtanga and Hatha yoga, and its teacher training is registered with Yoga Australia.

Graduates are trained to teach in Australia and internationally, and many now do.

Course overview

Our Level 1, 200 hour teacher training is a hands-on, practical course for yoga students, yoga teachers and dedicated practitioners who want deeper knowledge, and for beginners too. It is built on Sage Patanjali's Ashtanga yoga, from the Yoga Sutras, and Swami Swatmarama's Hatha yoga, from the Hatha Yoga Pradipika of 1200 AD, two of the oldest and most respected yoga texts.

The course runs in Melbourne on Sundays, so you can keep working and look after your family. When you finish, you receive a 200 hour teaching certificate recognised in Australia and overseas.

The Level 1, 200 hour syllabus

  • Watch the trainees reciting the Gayatri mantra

    Philosophy, mantra and Sanskrit

    Ashtanga and Hatha yoga, Patanjali's Yoga Sutras and their meaning, the Bhagavad Gita, and an introduction to the Vedas and Upanishads.

    You learn Sanskrit shlokas, mantras and chants: their meaning, pronunciation and how to use them in your classes. The video shows trainees reciting the Gayatri mantra.

  • Suryanamaskar and asana

    Suryanamaskar in levels, moving with the breath, done the traditional way with its mantra. More than 50 asanas, beginner to advanced, with alignment, modifications and safe sequencing for elderly people, adults, teens and kids.

    • Standing and balancing
    • Forward bends
    • Backward bends
    • Abdominal
    • Inversions
    • Sitting
    • Twists
    • Shavasana

  • Modern and yogic anatomy

    The nervous, endocrine, lymphatic, respiratory, cardiovascular, digestive and musculoskeletal systems, with biomechanics and alignment in the asanas.

    Then yogic anatomy: the chakras, the nadis that carry prana, and the bandhas. You learn self-correction and the precautions to take in asana and pranayama.

  • Pranayama

    Classical breathing techniques with bandhas and mudras: ujjayi (victory breath), nadi shodhana (alternate nostril), kapalabhati (breath of fire), sitali (cooling breath) and more.

    You learn how the breath directs prana, and the precautions for each technique.

  • Meditation

    Dharana and dhyana, using traditional techniques passed down for generations. You practise them during the training, going further than concentrating on an object or visualising.

  • Shatkarma, kundalini and karma yoga

    The cleansing techniques of traditional Hatha yoga, recorded around 1200 AD. Kundalini energy, which the practice works towards.

    Karma yoga from the Vedas, put to work in daily life, with self-enquiry into who you are and where you are going.

Practical teaching methodology

You are trained and mentored to teach asana and pranayama, keeping to the tradition and its precautions.

  • Preparing before you take a class
  • Leading a class of 15 and a class of 50 or more, with timing for each asana
  • Keeping a class alert and enjoyable from start to finish
  • Staying with the traditional system while you teach
  • Holding correct alignment as you demonstrate, and avoiding injury
  • Explaining each asana's benefits and precautions as you teach
  • Teaching beginners and regular students, together or apart
  • Teaching in a club and in a dedicated yoga class
  • Keeping your own practice going in a busy teaching week

  • Pre and post pregnancy

    The traditional methods used in these periods, with insight from our own teachers' case studies.

  • People with special needs

    How to take care of people with special needs in your classes.

  • Kids and teens

    How to teach school children and run a kids' class across age groups.

  • Private classes

    A session of its own on one-to-one classes, face to face and online.

  • Mock group teaching

    Every trainee leads mock classes within the group, gets feedback, and learns to observe others teaching.

  • A teacher's role

    The vision to bring to public classes, in a studio or a club, and how to become a successful teacher in your community.

  • Starting your own classes

    Advertising and marketing tips, and the stories of past trainees who now run their own studios.

  • Mentoring for teaching work

    How to apply for yoga jobs, with mentoring from the school's director for trainees who meet the course requirements.

Also covered: why yoga has so many style names, and what an Ashtanga or Hatha class should be according to the original teachings. Iyengar, Krama Vinyasa, Vinyasa and Kriya yoga are explained, along with newer names such as Yin, Power and Hot yoga.

What it takes to graduate

  • 200hours of training
  • 90%minimum attendance
  • 2rounds of practical and open-book theory exams

Graduates receive a 200-hour teaching certificate and are eligible for professional indemnity and public liability insurance in Australia and overseas.

The 350 Hour Level 2 adds 150 hours and is open to Level 1 graduates.

Questions about the Melbourne training

Do I need experience to do yoga teacher training?

No teaching experience is needed. Trainees are mentored from the basics, whatever their starting point. We ask for a commitment to attend every session.

Is the teacher training recognised by Yoga Australia?

Yes. It is a Yoga Australia registered course.

How long does the 200-hour training take?

Five months, on Sundays, so you can keep working. The exact schedule is confirmed when you enrol.

Can I teach yoga after the course?

Yes. You graduate with a 200-hour teaching certificate and are eligible for the insurance that gyms, community centres and studios ask for. Many graduates now teach in Melbourne, around Australia and overseas.

How much does the teacher training cost?

Fees vary by intake and format, and there is no early-bird price. Send an enquiry and we quote the Melbourne intake by email.

How is this school different?

We teach classical Ashtanga and Hatha from the source texts, without props. The principal teacher, Samir Rane, is a Registered Senior Teacher with Yoga Australia and the third generation of a yogic family, with more than 30 years in yoga and 16 years of teaching in Australia.

Can I do the teacher training online?

Yes. The online intake is live-streamed, with the same teachers and syllabus.
